Risk appetite

Measured on the names that carry risk, ranked out of the universe rather than picked. High beta is the top quintile by beta to the S&P over the equal-weight bottom quintile. Leaders are the top quintile by 6-month return, lagged a month so the cohort is not selected on the window it is scored over.
History
The line is high-beta over low-beta, rebased to the window start — falling means risk is being sold whatever the index does. The band underneath is leader breadth less index breadth: below zero, the index is being carried by names that were not leading it. That is what a defensive rotation looks like from the inside, and it is invisible to a breadth count.

Participation

S&P members, equal weight
How much of the index is above water. This is a trend measure, not a risk measure. Every name counts the same, so money leaving semiconductors for staples and utilities can leave these numbers high — or push them up.

      High tight flags

      TickerRunFlag PullbackFlag vol Off 52w high
      A double or better inside 40 sessions, peaking within 5% of the 52-week high, then holding within 25% of that peak for 10–35 sessions. Read the count, not the names. This universe is large caps, so the number of flags is a clean gauge of speculative appetite while the names it returns are big companies that happen to fit the geometry.
      History
      Flag count against the share of S&P names above their 50-day. They are not the same signal: a slow, broad advance puts most names above the average with no flags at all, while a hot, narrow one produces flags on a middling breadth reading.
